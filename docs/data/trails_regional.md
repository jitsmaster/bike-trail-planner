# Regional trail data (`trails_regional.json`)

GENERATED by `tools/build_regional_trails.py` -- do not edit by hand.
Authoritative copy: this directory. The app copy at
`app/src/main/assets/trails_regional.json` is produced by `tools/build_assets.py`.

Decision: `docs/DECISIONS.md` D23, final answer (a) -- bundle the regional
OpenStreetMap `route=bicycle` + `route=mtb` relations for Washington state + the
Portland metro and the northern Willamette Valley. The six near-home trails stay in
their own file (`trails.json`), because they are the only ones with measured traffic,
a closure check and a surface audit.

**Every trail in this file is UNVERIFIED (`verified = false`).** See
`docs/REGIONAL-TRAILS.md` for what that does and does not mean, for the safety reason
why road-following route relations are excluded, and for the before/after
measurements of the clipping fix.

## Selection rules (in order)

1. keep relations whose `type` is `route` and whose `route` is `bicycle` or `mtb`;
2. CLIP every geometry to the region = the union of the boxes `(45.53, -124.87, 49.01, -116.9), (45.0, -123.7, 46.0, -121.8)` (S, W, N, E):
   Washington state, the Portland metro and the northern Willamette Valley.
   **This step is what makes the lengths rideable.** A relation can list members far
   outside the region, and the Washington extract keeps whole relations, so without
   clipping the file held a coordinate range of lat 42.307..55.354, lon
   -131.714..-116.996 (Alaska) and three relations claiming 1,127 km each;
3. **SAFETY GATE -- off-road share >= 0.90.** The app grades trail geometry Grade A by
   construction, with no traffic data (`SafetyGrader.gradeTrailLeg`,
   `app/src/main/java/com/biketrailplanner/domain/SafetyGrader.kt:236`). That is only
   sound for a real off-road path, so a `route=bicycle` relation that is a signed
   route along roads is not bundled. Share is measured over the CLIPPED member ways;
   off-road = `highway` in bridleway, cycleway, footway, path, pedestrian, steps, track;
   on-road = living_street, motorway, motorway_link, primary, primary_link, residential, road, secondary, secondary_link, service, tertiary, tertiary_link, trunk, trunk_link, unclassified; anything
   else counts as unknown, and unknown is never off-road;
4. **SHIP OFF-ROAD GEOMETRY ONLY.** The on-road and unknown member pieces are removed
   from `segments`, so every shipped metre is off-road and the app's Grade A trail leg
   can never grade a road metre as trail. Pieces are never joined across a removed
   road piece, so a relation can ship as several rideable spans -- which the app's
   `Trail` already supports (`rideableSpans`, the Sumner Link closure case). A
   relation is kept only when at least **1.0 km** of off-road geometry remains;
   **24** relation(s) fell below that and were dropped. Total road geometry removed
   from the shipped shapes: **38.3 km** (Washington 32.1, Portland 6.2);
5. drop every clipped piece shorter than **250 m** (a leftover stub at a region
   edge), then drop the relation if its off-road total is under **1.0 km**;
6. drop any relation longer than **800 km** -- insurance against a membership
   artifact pretending to be a ride (the 1,127 km artifacts are handled by the
   clipping and the density guard). A LONG trail is fine: the app plans a 5-50 mile
   round trip (D20) and rides the part nearest the rider;
7. drop degenerate geometry (**fewer than 3 clipped points**: a straight line the
   trail walk cannot use);
8. drop geometry sparser than **500 m per point** (mean point spacing) on the
   CLIPPED SOURCE geometry, and sparser than **1500 m per point** on the SHIPPED
   simplified geometry -- Douglas-Peucker removes collinear points, so a real straight
   rail-trail is legitimately sparse once simplified (measured: 571-793 m/point),
   while a membership artifact is 2,600+ m/point;
9. drop a relation that the two sources both returned (the Portland bbox reaches into
   Clark County, WA), and a relation that duplicates another relation's exact points
   under a different id;
10. label an unnamed relation `Unnamed bike route (r<relation id>)` -- an empty name
    reaches the rider's screen and the rotation signature.

## Off-road share (the safety gate)

Median off-road share of a SHIPPED relation: **1.00** (Washington) / **1.00**
(Portland). Distribution over every relation in each source that had classable
geometry, before the cut -- this is why 0.90 was chosen:

|---|---|---|
| 0.0-0.1 | 129 | 88 |
| 0.1-0.2 | 21 | 6 |
| 0.2-0.3 | 10 | 5 |
| 0.3-0.4 | 6 | 0 |
| 0.4-0.5 | 3 | 2 |
| 0.5-0.6 | 11 | 1 |
| 0.6-0.7 | 14 | 2 |
| 0.7-0.8 | 10 | 8 |
| 0.8-0.9 | 24 | 9 |
| 0.9-1.0 | 185 | 41 |

The distribution is dominated by two clusters -- a real path network (0.9-1.0) and a
route along streets (0.0-0.1) -- with a real middle band between them (Washington: 59
relations at 0.5-0.9; Portland: 20). The cut is at **0.90**, which was chosen because
the grader is all-or-nothing: it marks the WHOLE trail leg Grade A, so every on-road
km inside a bundled relation is a road km graded as separated trail.

- At 0.90 a 30 km trail may carry 3 km of street and still be bundled. The 33
  relations in the 0.80-0.90 band were dropped for that reason; loosening the cut to
  0.80 would add them back, at the cost of grading up to 20% of their length as if it
  were a path.
- The band below 0.50 (270 relations) is a route along streets, not a trail, and is
  never bundled.

### Shipped geometry is 100% off-road

Rule 4 removes the road pieces, so the invariant holds by construction: no shipped
metre is a road metre. The table below is the mix of the SOURCE relations that passed
the gate; the removed column is what rule 4 took out of the shapes:

| | Washington | Portland metro | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| off-road member-way km (source measure) | 2116.9 | 358.2 | 2475.1 |
| on-road member-way km | 29.8 | 6.2 | 36.0 |
| unknown `highway` km | 2.3 | 0.0 | 2.3 |
| **removed from the shapes** | 32.1 | 6.2 | 38.3 |

Worked example of what the gate costs, and why it is worth it: **Hagg Lake Trail**
(Portland) has 18.85 km off-road and 2.63 km on the park loop road, a share of 0.877, so
it is excluded -- at 0.90 an absolute invariant, those 2.63 km would have been graded
Grade A trail. The same is true of Washington Timber Trail (0.31) and Olympic
Discovery Trail (0.51), which is why neither is in the file even though the 800 km cap
would now keep them.

The 0.80-0.90 band stays excluded. That is a **deliberate choice, not an oversight**:
`gradeTrailLeg` is all-or-nothing, so admitting that band would grade up to 20% of
those routes as if they were separated trail. Coverage is not the constraint (this
bundle already has 191 trails and 2447 km); honesty of the grade is.

## Simplification measurements

Douglas-Peucker, applied per geometry piece, over the shipped set:

| tolerance | geometry points | reduction | total km | max deviation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10.0 m | 14550 | 81.9% | 2446.62 | 10.0 m |
| 20.0 m | 9358 | 88.3% | 2428.08 | 20.0 m |
| (clipped, unsimplified) | 80201 | -- | 2467.74 | -- |

**Chosen tolerance: 10.0 m.** 10 m chosen over 20 m: 20 m would save only about 0.3 MB and the file is inside the few-MB budget either way, while 10 m halves the length error (0.44% short of the source geometry against 0.80%) and keeps the drawn shape on the correct side of a junction, because 10 m is about one lane width.

## Records

One record per relation, with these fields:

- `id`
- `name`
- `route`
- `surface`
- `region`
- `length_km_clipped`
- `length_km_source_unclipped`
- `offroad_share`
- `offroad_km`
- `onroad_km_removed`
- `loop`
- `verified`
- `segments`

Exactly what the length and shape fields mean:

- `length_km_clipped` -- measured on the SIMPLIFIED geometry after clipping to the region: the length the app may use
- `length_km_source_unclipped` -- the whole relation as assembled from the source, before clipping: provenance only, never rideable length
- `segments` -- one or more contiguous chains of [lat, lon]. EVERY metre of these is off-road: on-road and unknown-highway member pieces are removed at build time and never joined across, so the app (which grades trail geometry Grade A by construction) cannot grade a road metre as trail. Pieces may therefore be several rideable spans, like the Sumner Link closure case.
- `onroad_km_removed` -- clipped road geometry that belonged to this relation in OSM and was removed from `segments`
- `offroad_share` -- share of the SOURCE relation's CLIPPED member-way length whose highway class is off-road (cycleway/path/footway/bridleway/track/pedestrian/steps). Everything below 0.90 is not bundled at all, and inside a bundled relation only the off-road part reaches `segments`.
- `offroad_km` -- the off-road part of offroad_share, in km

So `length_km_clipped` is the rideable length inside the region, and
`length_km_source_unclipped` is the whole relation as the source holds it. `loop` is
true when the largest piece starts and ends within 100 m of itself.
